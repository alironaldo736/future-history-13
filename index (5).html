Learning About Indigenous People

This website was made for Grade 11 NBE 3U, Media Studies. We are looking at TV shows that show Indigenous people in a good way.

We study the episode "Awaken" from the TV show Future History. This show tells real stories about Indigenous people and their lives. The episode shows how family, culture, and teachings help them know who they are. It teaches us why Indigenous traditions matter and why we should respect them.

Introduction to Future History

Future History is a TV show made by Indigenous people that explores Indigenous culture and history. It connects traditions from the past to life today.

About the Show

  • Creator: Jennifer Podemski (Anishinaabe/Ashkenazi Jewish)
  • Writer: Tamara Podemski (Anishinaabe/Ashkenazi Jewish)
  • Hosted by: Kris Nahrgang & Sarain Fox
  • Released: 2019 (Season 2, Episode 1)

"Future History shows Indigenous people as modern, smart, and looking to the future. It breaks old stereotypes and respects traditional knowledge."

About the "Awaken" Episode

This episode is about finding out who you are as an Indigenous person. The hosts talk to different people about:

  • How family helps you understand where you come from
  • How cultural practices (like making medicine bundles) connect you to your traditions
  • How learning from elders helps you find your identity
  • How land and nature play a big part in Indigenous teaching

The episode shows that being Indigenous isn't just about the past. It's about keeping traditions alive today and passing them to future generations.

What Happens in "Awaken"

The main parts of the show and what they mean.

Family Roots

Kris talks with his uncle and mother to learn about his family history. He learns how past rules and laws affected his family.

"Learning where I come from helps me understand who I am today." - Kris

Medicine Bundle

Sarain makes a medicine bundle with help from her mother. This shows how spiritual traditions are still important today.

"These medicines carry the wisdom of many generations." - Sarain

Land Teachings

Both hosts learn from Dr. Deb about land and spirit. She teaches them that people and nature are connected and must take care of each other.

"The land remembers what people forget." - Dr. Deb

What the Show is About

In "Awaken," hosts Kris and Sarain explore what it means to be Indigenous and to belong to a community with deep roots in history. The show begins with both hosts talking about their own journeys of learning about their culture.

During the show, they visit different Indigenous communities. They talk with elders, knowledge keepers, and young people who share stories about how they've kept their cultural identities even though there were many attempts in the past to erase Indigenous cultures through schools and other policies.

The show also looks at how traditional practices like storytelling, art, language, and ceremonies help connect people to their ancestors and give them a sense of belonging. Elders explain how these practices carry wisdom that has been passed down and is still important today.

By the end of the show, viewers understand that Indigenous identity is always growing and changing, while staying connected to traditional knowledge and community. The message is that connecting to your cultural heritage is a lifelong journey that makes both individuals and communities stronger.

Important Parts of the Show

The most important scenes in the "Awaken" episode and why they matter.

1. Personal Stories

At the start, Kris and Sarain share their own stories about being Indigenous. They talk about how they feel connected to their culture and history. This shows that finding your identity is a journey that happens over time.

The camera shows close-ups of their faces when they share emotional moments. The music uses traditional Indigenous instruments to help viewers connect with the feelings being shared.

2. Learning from Elders

An elder talks to young Indigenous people about their identity and where they come from. The elder explains how knowing your roots helps you face today's challenges with more strength.

The camera shows everyone sitting together, which helps us see how important community is. The lighting changes from daylight to firelight, showing how these teachings connect the past and present.

3. Community Ceremony

The show includes a traditional ceremony with people of all ages taking part. This scene shows how ceremonies help Indigenous people feel connected to each other and to their ancestors.

The filmmakers film from a respectful distance to honor the sacred nature of the ceremony. The sounds mix nature and drumming to help viewers feel like they are there.

How the Show Tells Stories

Looking at how the show uses filmmaking techniques to share Indigenous views.

Visual Elements

The show uses camera work that respects Indigenous traditions. The filmmakers often show people with nature in the background, which helps us see how important land is to Indigenous identity.

There are many close-up shots of hands doing traditional crafts. These shots help viewers see how knowledge is passed down from older to younger generations.

The colors in the show change depending on the scene. Warm colors are used for community gatherings to make them feel happy and welcoming. Cooler colors are used when talking about history to help viewers feel more thoughtful.

Story Pattern

Instead of telling stories in a straight line (beginning, middle, end), "Awaken" uses a circular storytelling style that matches Indigenous traditions. The episode starts and ends with similar ideas, showing how knowledge moves in cycles.

The show mixes personal stories with community events. This creates a web of different voices that shows both individual experiences and shared knowledge.

This way of telling stories is different from most documentaries and helps viewers understand Indigenous ways of sharing information, where stories build on each other rather than following a strict timeline.

Indigenous People on TV

How "Awaken" shows real Indigenous stories in a good way.

Indigenous People Make This Show

Future History is special because Indigenous people make the show. They choose how to tell their own stories. They worked with Indigenous communities to make sure the show is true and shows respect.

The show doesn't use wrong ideas about Indigenous people that you might see on other TV shows. It shows many different Indigenous lives in Canada. It also shows what many groups share.

By having Indigenous people both in front of the camera and making the show behind the scenes, "Awaken" lets Indigenous communities tell their own stories instead of having others tell stories about them.

What We Learned

The main things we learned from watching "Awaken".

Main Points

Finding Your Identity: "Awaken" shows that Indigenous identity is a journey of learning and connecting that continues across generations.

Community Matters: The episode shows how your identity is connected to your community and family, which help you understand who you are.

Passing Down Knowledge: Traditional practices, stories, and ceremonies are ways to pass important knowledge about values and ways of seeing the world from one generation to the next.

Better TV Shows: When Indigenous people create their own shows like "Future History," it helps fight stereotypes and gives communities the power to tell their own stories.
